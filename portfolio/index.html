Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

I think I did a good job of leveraging technology and tools that were new to me in creating something user-friendly and visually appealing. I’ve learned that it is crucial to understand how others perceive your work and to optimize designs based on feedback. I have also been able to apply my technical writing skills, specifically in the Good Design project, to convey a direct message. Rhetoric and word choice are extremely important in getting a point across - especially when explaining something visual that can’t always speak for itself.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

I definitely learned to take design risks while working on these assignments. I used a lot of tools I wasn’t, and am still not entirely, comfortable with. I learned what makes good design “good” and applied it to my own work. Through trial and error I managed to get some results I am pretty proud of.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

I made sure to bring something to workshop every class (although I didn’t always submit it on time). I learned a lot from my peers. We helped each other learn how to use different applications, fix things that weren’t working, and gave each other overall inspiration on how we could make our designs better. It was a lot of fun seeing what my group would bring in each day and was ultimately super helpful to have people to troubleshoot my problems with.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Revision is a critical part of the design process, and I made sure it was included in my personal process each time I made something new. Some projects required more revisions than others, like my Cartoon, but I always made sure to think about what principles I could apply to make my design better before submitting. The Logos project probably taught me the most about revision and really forced me to go through the process step-by-step.

5. Learn to present your work.

I made a good effort to incorporate the design principles we learned in class into my work. Most notably, I utilized contrast, alignment, and font choice. There is always room for more revision and application of skills, but I am proud of what I learned and how I managed to apply it in these projects.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making:

For these short form projects, I continued to build on the technological skills I have gained throughout the semester. I have learned a lot about how to utilize rhetoric in creating designs that are inclusive and accessible. I was able to successfully incorporate principles of design in aligning my text and writing suscinctly to create products that are accessible to a wide range of audiences. Ethics in design ties very closely to accessibility and inclusion. We want as many people as possible to be able to enjoy what we create.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

I have continued to take risks with my designs and become more comfortable with software like InDesign and Photoshop. I have learned how to apply design principles to serve a specific audience. I now understand how aspects of design like contrast and text alignment impact reading experience and overall accessibility.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Workshops have been critical to revising my work and making it the best it can be. I feel like I have really gotten to know the people around me through their designs. We have learned a lot from each other during workshop, from how our designs are perceived by others to how to better use different tools. Even when I brought something incomplete to class, I still feel that I received very valuable insight from my peers.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

I have learned a lot about my own design process through revision. I am typically a person who likes to immediately produce a final result. Taking time away from a project and coming back to look at it a second (or third or fourth) time is one of the most critical components of the design process. I have come to appreciate the insight I can give myself after stepping away from a project and coming back to it.

I am still learning to get more comfortable presenting my work to the class. Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

These long-form projects forced me to consider the roles of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in creating designs - especially when working with clients or getting feedback from others. I had to really think about how my words and use of design elements would impact others - not just my client, but also my client's client.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

I have been learning new design principles all semester. These long form projects forced me to apply and leverage them in real-world scenarios. When my non-designer friend or client would give me feedback on my work, I would often know which design elements to use in order to fix the given problem. I learned to use design principles as tools for problem solving rather than simply tools for building.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Peer feedback throughout these final projects were critcal to my end results. My group members were able to help each other out as design processes became more complex. It's really beneficial to receive feedback from people who are working on similarly structured projects.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

The revision process was much more taxing during the long form projects. Because completing these designs felt like such a feat, it was hard to go back and make the necessary changes knowing how long I had spent on the original. This difficulty was ultimately beneficial to me in that it forced me to get over my aversion to revision. It is a necessary part of the process - not an entirely new process itself.

5. Learn to present your work.

By the end of the semester, I felt completely comfortable sharing my in-progress work with my group and asking for help with tools I wasn't exactly sure how to use. When we presented our UX Branding Projects, I felt more confident than I had all semester in giving and receiving feedback.
